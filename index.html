    Questions

    Is this really live?

    The positions are worked out in your browser for the current second, using the same SGP4 orbit model that tracking centres publish their data for. The orbit data itself is a snapshot: it's updated regularly. The clock on the page tells you what moment you're looking at.

    How accurate is it?

    For a fresh orbit, typically within a few kilometres. The error grows by a kilometre or more a day, and faster for very low satellites or ones that have just changed course. It's more than enough to find one in the sky, and not good enough to dock with.

    What does "active" leave out?

    Rocket bodies, dead satellites and debris. Trackers follow tens of thousands more of those, and they're not shown here. Some classified satellites aren't in this public list either.

    Why are some dots dimmer?

    They're in Earth's shadow. The shadow is worked out from where the Sun really is now. The day and night on the globe come from the same sum.

    Does it know where I am?

    It guesses a nearby city from your device's time zone, and nothing leaves your browser. "Use my location" asks your browser's permission first, and your position is only used on this page.

    Can I use the keyboard?

    Yes. Every control is a real button or field. With a satellite chosen, the arrow keys step through the list, and Escape puts it back.
